## 1. The AI ROI measurement problem: acute, well-documented, and unsolved

The single most consistent finding across every 2025â€“2026 survey is a widening gap between AI coding adoption (near-universal) and the ability to measure its impact (rare and immature).

**Adoption is saturated.** Jellyfish's 2025 State of Engineering Management report (645 respondents across ICs, managers, executives; published July 8, 2025) found 90% of engineering teams now use AI coding tools, up from 61% a year earlier; only 3% have no plans to. 62% *believe* they're getting at least a 25% velocity increase; fewer than 1% believe AI slows them down ([jellyfish.co](https://jellyfish.co/newsroom/software-development-to-shift-from-humans-to-ai-jellyfish-report-finds/)) **[HARD survey data, self-reported perceptions]**. Gartner predicts 90% of enterprise software engineers will use AI code assistants by 2028, up from under 14% in early 2024 ([gartner.com](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-01-gartner-identifies-the-top-strategic-trends-in-software-engineering-for-2025-and-beyond)) **[MED analyst forecast]**.

**Measurement is not.** The same Jellyfish report found only **20% of teams use engineering metrics to measure AI impact** â€” a 70-point gap between adoption and accountability ([jellyfish.co blog](https://jellyfish.co/blog/2025-software-engineering-management-trends/)) **[HARD]**. This gap is the core buyer pain.

**The perceptionâ€“reality gap is now scientifically documented.** METR's randomized controlled trial (Febâ€“Jun 2025; 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 real tasks, primarily Cursor Pro + Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet) found developers were **19% slower** with AI on mature codebases â€” while forecasting a 24% speedup and, even after the fact, believing they'd been 20% faster ([metr.org](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)) **[HARD â€” RCT, though small n and specific setting]**. This ~40-point perception gap is the strongest single argument that self-reported time savings (the metric most orgs use) cannot be trusted, and that objective, repo-specific measurement has real economic value.

**Objective telemetry shows modest, volatile gains.** DX (GetDX)'s longitudinal analysis of 400+ companies found a **median PR throughput increase of 7.76%** over a period when AI usage rose 65%, with most companies landing at 5â€“15% ([getdx.com](https://getdx.com/blog/measure-ai-impact/)) **[HARD telemetry]**. DX's Q1 2026 AI-assisted engineering report shows ~28% of committed code is now AI-authored and 93% industry adoption, but quality impact is "varied and volatile" â€” change failure rates swinging by up to 2 percentage points, meaning some companies ship 50% more defects than before ([getdx.com](https://getdx.com/blog/ai-measurement-framework-guide/)) **[HARD]**. Faros AI's July 2025 "AI Productivity Paradox" report (telemetry from 1,255 teams, 10,000+ developers) found AI-using developers complete **98% more code changes and 21% more tasks â€” but any correlation with performance evaporates at the company level** across throughput, DORA, and quality KPIs ([faros.ai](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering)) **[HARD telemetry]**. Bain's 2025 Technology Report echoes this: two-thirds of software firms rolled out genAI, gains of 10â€“15% typically don't convert to positive returns without workflow redesign; the best performers pairing AI with process transformation get 25â€“30% ([bain.com](https://www.bain.com/insights/from-pilots-to-payoff-generative-ai-in-software-development-technology-report-2025/)) **[MED consultant survey]**. A 2024 McKinsey survey found 8 in 10 companies report no material genAI earnings impact **[MED]**.

**CFO pressure is explicit.** The trigger is the mid-2026 repricing of AI coding tools: Anthropic restructured pricing in February 2026, OpenAI moved Codex to token metering in April, and GitHub moved **all Copilot plans to usage-based billing effective June 1, 2026**; procurement firm NPI projected 2â€“3x TCO increases for heavy users ([admios.com](https://www.admios.com/blog-posts/end-of-subsidized-ai-coding)) **[MED]**. DX now ships an "AI dollar impact" report translating AI investment into a net dollar figure explicitly "for board-level reporting and budget conversations" â€” the default inputs ($150K fully-loaded FTE, 65% dev time) show exactly which conversation buyers are having ([docs.getdx.com](https://docs.getdx.com/reports/ai-dollar-impact/)) **[HARD product evidence of demand]**. Atlassian's 2025 State of DevEx (3,500 developers/managers, six countries, fielded March 2025) adds the paradox fuel: 99% of developers report AI time savings and 68% save 10+ hours/week â€” yet 50% *lose* 10+ hours/week to organizational friction, and 63% say leaders don't understand their pain points (up from 44%) ([atlassian.com](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)) **[HARD survey]**. Atlassian notes developers spend only ~16% of time actually coding, capping what coding assistants alone can deliver.

**Developer trust is falling as usage rises.** Stack Overflow's 2025 survey (49,009 respondents, 166â€“177 countries): 84% use or plan to use AI tools (up from 76%), but trust in accuracy fell to ~29â€“33% from ~40â€“43%; 46% actively distrust it; the #1 frustration (66%) is AI output that's "almost right, but not quite," and 45% say debugging AI code takes longer than writing it ([survey.stackoverflow.co](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)) **[HARD survey]**.

## 2. How enterprises actually choose AI coding tools

**Tool sprawl is the norm, not the exception.** IDC's 2025 AI spend tracker puts the average enterprise (1,000+ employees) at **11.4 AI software vendors, up from 4.2 in early 2024**; McKinsey found 78% of enterprises use AI but only 27% have formal vendor governance ([supalabs.co summary](https://supalabs.co/en/blog/ai-vendor-consolidation-enterprise-guide-2026/)) **[MED â€” secondary compilation of IDC/McKinsey]**. Jellyfish found **48% of engineering teams use two or more AI coding tools**, with leaders explicitly taking a "diversified, exploratory approach over standardizing" ([jellyfish.co](https://jellyfish.co/blog/ai-impact-framework/)) **[HARD]**. Zapier's enterprise survey (500+ leaders): 28% use more than 10 AI applications; 66% plan to increase tool count; 39% have tried or plan to standardize on fewer ([zapier.com](https://zapier.com/blog/ai-sprawl-survey/)) **[MED vendor survey]**.

**Standardization is failing; bakeoffs and governance layers are winning.** Market share churns too fast for single-vendor mandates: Ramp corporate-spend data shows Cursor's category share fell from 41% (June 2025) to ~26% (May 2026) while Anthropic took roughly half ([cnbc.com](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/spacex-spcx-cursor-acquisition-ipo.html)) **[HARD spend data]**. The 2026 buyer playbook that emerges from procurement guides: run 2-day bakeoffs **on your own internal codebases**, standardize the security/audit layer (audit schema, model pinning, allowlists) rather than the tool, and let teams pick anything that clears the screen â€” because blanket mandates produce shadow installs that evade the audit trail ([particula.tech buyer's guide](https://particula.tech/blog/enterprise-ai-coding-agent-buyers-guide-2026)) **[MED practitioner guidance]**. Gartner's first dedicated Magic Quadrant for AI Code Assistants (Sept 15, 2025; 14 vendors, 5 Leaders: GitHub, AWS, GitLab, Google Cloud, Cognition) is itself procurement evidence â€” and Gartner sized the 2025 AI code assistant market at **$3.0â€“3.5B** ([visualstudiomagazine.com](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2025/09/17/report-github-tops-ai-coding-assistants-with-microsoft-related-cautions.aspx)) **[MED]**.

**Shadow AI is rampant.** Harness's State of Software Delivery found **52% of developers don't use IT-approved tools**; Software AG found 75% of knowledge workers use AI with >50% on personal/non-company tools ([itpro.com](https://www.itpro.com/software/development/shadow-ai-is-creeping-its-way-into-software-development-more-than-half-of-developers-admit-to-using-unauthorized-ai-tools-at-work-and-its-putting-companies-at-risk)) **[MED vendor surveys]**. IDC's April 2025 global employee survey: 39% of EMEA employees use free AI tools at work, 17% privately paid, only 23% use employer-provided tools ([idc.com](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/shadow-ai-how-stealth-productivity-is-strangling-enterprise-ai-adoption-and-creating-a-security-nightmare/)) **[HARD survey]**. Only ~12â€“18% of orgs have formal AI security policies or can identify all AI tools in use (Salesforce/Gartner figures via [redteampartner.com](https://redteampartner.com/blog/shadow-ai-enterprise-risk/)) **[MED secondary]**.

**Budget mechanics.** Jellyfish: 38% of companies fund AI tools as net-new spend, 23% reallocate headcount budget; 61% of engineering budgets grew as a share of revenue YoY **[HARD]**. Enterprise seat prices: Cursor implied ACV ~$2,000/user blended, enterprise contracts $5,000â€“15,000/seat; buyers negotiate 20â€“35% off list with committed seats ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/spacex-cursor-60b-acquisition-2026), [renewalpad.com](https://www.renewalpad.com/insights/ai-tool-sprawl-procurement-playbook-2026)) **[MED]**.

## 4. AI code quality and security: the verification gap is quantified

**GitClear (211M changed lines, 2020â€“2024, repos from Google/Microsoft/Meta + enterprise customers):** copy/pasted lines rose from 8.3% to 12.3% of changes; moved/refactored lines collapsed from ~25% to under 10% (projected ~3% for 2025); code churn (lines reverted/rewritten within 2 weeks) rose from 3.1% to 5.7% (7.9% of newly added code by another measure, vs 5.5% in 2020); duplicated 5+ line blocks up ~8â€“10x in two years. **Veracode (100+ LLMs, 80 tasks, 4 languages):** only **55% of AI-generated code is secure** without security prompting â€” and this has been flat from 2023 through the Spring 2026 update even as syntax correctness climbed from ~50% to 95%+. **Apiiro (Fortune 50 telemetry, Dec 2024â€“Jun 2025):** AI-assisted developers commit 3â€“4x faster; monthly security findings rose ~10x (from ~1,000 to 10,000+); privilege escalation paths +322%, architectural design flaws +153% â€” while syntax errors fell 76% and logic bugs fell 60% (making the danger less visible) ([CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-generated-code-vulnerability-surge-2026/)) **[HARD telemetry]**. ## 8. Governance and control-plane demand

## Synthesis: who would actually pay for repo-specific benchmarking / AI dev observability / PR verification â€” and from what budget

**1. The "prove the ROI" buyer â€” VP Engineering / CTO with CFO pressure. Confidence: HIGH.**
The evidence stack is unusually coherent: 90%+ adoption, only 20% measuring, a scientifically demonstrated ~40-point perception gap (METR), median real gains of ~8% vs perceived 25%+, and a 2026 usage-based-billing shock that turned AI tooling from flat SaaS into a variable, CFO-visible line item that can 2â€“3x. DX's $1B exit to Atlassian proves the measurement layer commands strategic value. Budget source: the existing developer-productivity/platform-engineering budget (the $50Kâ€“120K/yr Jellyfish-class line), increasingly co-sponsored by finance for board reporting.
