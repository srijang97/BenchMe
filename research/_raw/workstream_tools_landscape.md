### 3.1 Multi-tool usage is the norm, and it's operationally messy

### 3.2 Usage-based billing pain is now universal â€” every major vendor has had its own scandal

The pattern repeats with striking consistency: **Cursor June 2025** (apology + refunds), **Anthropic Aug 2025** (weekly caps â†’ June 2026 class action), **Replit Sept 2025** ($1K/week bills), **JetBrains 2025** (quota exhausted "in hours"), **OpenAI April 2026** (token-credit migration, 600-comment burn-rate issue), **GitHub June 2026** (day-one credit exhaustion, $180 bills, removed fallback model). The common failure modes: (1) "unlimited" that's conditional (Cursor Auto-mode, Copilot completions-only); (2) no real-time meter at launch; (3) agentic workloads consuming tokens orders of magnitude faster than chat, which subscription pricing was calibrated for; (4) defaults that are dangerous (Copilot's additional-usage budget defaults to *unbounded*; Devin's auto-reload on by default). Vendors' converging answer is credits+top-ups+budget controls (GitHub, JetBrains, Cursor, GitLab, Factory) and quota systems marketed as "predictable" (Windsurf/Devin Desktop daily/weekly quotas). The strategic consequence: **BYOK/open-source agents are the pressure valve** â€” BYOK doubled to 36% of surveyed devs in three months, and JetBrains itself now ships BYOK as the fix for its own credit complaints **[MEDIUM]**.

### 3.3 Enterprise governance gaps

What platform teams say they can't see or control: which tools/agents are in use at all (shadow AI, incl. Vendors differentiating on governance: Factory (OTEL telemetry to customer infra, air-gap), GitLab (Bedrock/IAM reuse), OpenHands (self-hosted, RBAC, budget controls), Cline enterprise (provider allowlists), Qodo (codified review policy).

### 3.4 Procurement pain / choosing is genuinely hard

### 3.5 PR review noise

### 3.6 Telemetry/analytics is becoming a product category
